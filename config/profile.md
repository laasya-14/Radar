# My taste profile

This is the main thing Radar uses to judge content. Edit it freely — it's sent to the
judge model on every scoring run. (The "Learned preferences" section on the Taste page is
written by the system from your feedback and is appended after this.)

## What I want
- Work that **changes something fundamentally**: a new idea, a new technique, a surprising
  result, a creative approach, a deep explanation of how something actually works.
- Material that **builds fundamentals** or gets me up to speed in a field quickly —
  especially in fields I'm newer to (robotics, astronomy/astrophysics). Clear explainers,
  good surveys, first-principles write-ups, lecture notes, "how X really works".
- **How strong engineers learn and work** right now: their setups, workflows, and habits —
  but only when there's a real insight I can learn from, not a gadget list.
- **Where engineering is heading** in the age of AI: thoughtful, experience-based takes on how
  the craft, tooling, languages and careers are changing.
- Primary sources over coverage: the paper, the repo, the author's own post.
- **Intersections excite me most**: quantum computing × cryptography × cybersecurity
  (post-quantum crypto, quantum cryptanalysis, migration in real systems), and
  astronomy/astrophysics/physics × computer science (simulations, scientific computing,
  telescope software and data, ML/AI for science). Rank work that bridges these fields higher.

## Whose work I trust
- Professors and researchers at top universities and labs, or with a strong track record.
- Engineers at companies (big or small, established or early) doing **fundamental** work —
  infra, compilers, networking, models, robotics hardware, security research.
- Serious open-source maintainers/contributors and genuine hackers who build things.

## What I don't want
- **Wrappers**: thin products that glue an LLM API onto an existing workflow, "ChatGPT for X",
  prompt collections, yet-another agent framework with nothing new inside.
- Generic commentators/influencers: "this is huge", "X is dead", "open source this/that",
  threads of hot takes, engagement bait, listicles, "10 tools you need".
- Funding/valuation/drama news, launch PR without technical substance, benchmark-chasing
  announcements with no insight.
- Setup/productivity posts that are just a list of apps.
- **Promotion and monetised hype**: people whose goal is to sell or grow an audience off new
  things — course/cohort sellers, "DM me", paid-newsletter/funnel pushers, affiliate links,
  "I made $X with AI", consultants farming leads, sponsored posts, company marketing dressed
  up as insight, launch threads whose point is the sign-up link. If the main purpose of a
  piece is to sell something or grow the author's following, I don't want it — even if the
  topic is right.

## Format preferences
- Fine with long, dense material if it's high quality. Papers are welcome.
- For robotics and astronomy: prefer things a smart engineer without domain background can
  learn from (but not dumbed-down pop-science).
